Starting from [[Compounding learning — forgetting as depreciation (spark)]].

Treat learning achievement as an asset: each day's study is a **deposit**, forgetting is **depreciation**.
So the effective growth rate is:

> net growth rate = learning rate − forgetting rate

- learning rate > forgetting rate → it compounds (real exponential gains)
- learning rate ≤ forgetting rate → you stall, or decay

The "1% a day" fantasy breaks exactly when the forgetting rate overtakes the learning rate.
That reframes spaced repetition: it isn't a lever to *add more*, it's a lever to *forget less* —
cutting the leak is often cheaper than pouring in more water.

Open question: is the forgetting rate constant? The Ebbinghaus curve is nonlinear, so it may be a function
of review spacing rather than a flat depreciation. → expand into a permanent note.
